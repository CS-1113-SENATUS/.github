Thanks everyone for joining tonight! Here's what we covered:

- **The team**: Sebastián, Professor Yitzchak Novick, and Professor Lucas O'Rourke (joining CS 1113 for the first time) are teaching this semester. Head CAs are Ian (office hours) and Leo (lab). Lab pairings for the semester: Lab 1 @Leonardo Villalobos & @Maria Fraguas; Lab 2 @Vincy Chen & @Wesley Zhuang; Lab 3 @Mandy Wang & @Maryam Alali; Lab 4 @Summer Thym & @Ian; Lab 5 @Matthew Jiang, @Natt Hong & @Salvador Baray. Every pairing has at least one returning CA. Use the dedicated Slack channel for anything lab-specific that doesn't need the whole team.
- **Team motto**: "Positive altruism": the goal isn't grades, it's making sure students feel zero shame struggling with genuinely hard material. Be patient, be human, don't be mean.
- **Communication (Slack only)**: Discord is being sunset. Keep notifications on and respond in a timely manner starting now. Don't let students email you directly—forward anything to Ian. Don't answer outside your posted hours; nobody here works for free.
- **Course Community app**: Replacing both Rephactor (student exercises) and Brightspace announcements/EdStem. Brightspace stays only for reference material; the app is for push-style important announcements and Q&A. You'll only see questions from your own cohort (~15 students). Full app/attendance/RAG Race training is Session 2.
- **Office hours**: Not babysitting; help guide students to course material, don't hand out solutions, don't check homework for correctness (only discuss specific conceptual questions). If you're running >15 min late, notify the students in the room, not necessarily the whole team.
- **Test corrections**: Students can regain up to 40% of lost points, in person only (no notes, no AI during the session), fully at CA discretion—you decide if they understand it, not just whether they got it right. This also applies to lab quizzes, not just midterms, so students get a full practice cycle before Midterm 1.
- **Labs**: Take attendance immediately (via app/QR code), present the weekly review slideshow (posted in Drive/GitHub, draft Tue, final by Thu), take questions, then run recitation. Lab quizzes are 30 min, standardised to the end of lab, and test the previous week's material, not the current week's. Cadence is irregular, so tell students to check the syllabus rather than assume "every other week."
- **Cheating**: Red flags for AI use—f-strings before they're taught, excessive/over-explained comments, stray non-code text pasted into `.py` files. If suspicious, confirm with another CA before acting, then quietly ask the student to explain their work rather than calling them out publicly. Always report to @Sebastián.
- **Grading**: All done in Gradescope with a pre-built rubric (@Ian builds it after grading the first 10–15 submissions this semester, rather than CAs building it live as before). Grade assuming any earlier small mistake was fixed correctly. Post any rubric questions/additions in the new #rubrics Slack channel.
- **Difficult students & Title IX**: Not your job to manage difficult students—escalate to a professor immediately. Any report of sexual misconduct/harassment (witnessed or disclosed to you, from anyone) must be reported. This is a legal obligation, not optional. Know where the emergency contact sheet is at each podium; you are the one calling 911 in a medical emergency.
- **Attendance policy change**: Students below 80% lecture attendance auto-fail, no exceptions. No lab makeups this semester—one dropped lab grade, period (previous excused-absence system was being gamed).
- **Extra credit homework**: 60–79.9% average == 3% extra credit; 80%+ == 5%, no questions asked. 90%+ similarity between two students auto-disqualifies from extra credit only (no department report, no appeal).
